Section 1

## Understanding Abortion

**Abortion** is the ending of a pregnancy. **Spontaneous abortion** is the medical term for miscarriage, which happens naturally. **Induced abortion** is a deliberate medical or surgical intervention to end a pregnancy. Abortion is a medical, social and ethical issue because it touches health systems, laws, personal circumstances and moral beliefs all at once.

### Medication abortion

Uses prescribed medicines (WHO-recommended regimens) to end an early pregnancy.

### Procedural abortion

A clinical procedure (such as vacuum aspiration) performed by trained providers. Method depends on pregnancy duration.

### Safe vs unsafe

WHO calls abortion very safe when done with a recommended method, suited to the pregnancy duration, by a skilled person. Unsafe abortion is a major preventable cause of maternal harm.

### Pregnancy development timeline (general overview)

**Weeks 1–4** Fertilisation, implantation, pregnancy hormones begin.

**Weeks 5–12** First trimester: major organs begin forming; heartbeat activity can be detected by ultrasound.

**Weeks 13–27** Second trimester: rapid growth; the pregnancy becomes visible.

**Weeks 28–40** Third trimester: lungs and brain mature before birth.

Fact or opinion? When life or personhood begins is a philosophical and moral question, not something biology alone settles. This exhibition keeps scientific facts and ethical opinions separate.

## Bodily Autonomy & Reproductive Rights

Bodily autonomy is the principle that people decide what happens to their own bodies, based on informed consent. In pregnancy it raises hard questions about personal freedom, responsibility and human dignity. Tap a concept:

### Health

Access to contraception, family planning and safe care.

### Equality

Decision-making power affects education, work and gender equality.

### Dignity & privacy

Accurate information and confidential care.

### Public health

Safe services reduce deaths; governments and health systems shape access.

## Moral & Ethical Arguments

Thoughtful people disagree. None of these views is ignorant, immoral or automatically correct.

| Perspective | Main arguments | Ethical questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supporting abortion rights** | Bodily autonomy; personal choice; health and safety of the pregnant person; socioeconomic circumstances; equality and reproductive justice. | Can anyone be required to continue a pregnancy? How do laws affect the vulnerable? |
| **Opposing abortion** | Moral status of the fetus; value of potential human life; religious and philosophical beliefs; moral responsibilities of pregnancy. | When does human life gain full moral protection? What do we owe the unborn? |
| **Middle-ground** | Views vary by circumstance and stage; balance maternal autonomy with fetal moral status; exceptions for serious health risk and other circumstances. | Do moral weights change over time? Which exceptions are justified, and who decides? |

## Medical Facts, Risks & Care

### Qualified care matters

WHO states that safety depends on method, duration and provider skill. Risks (such as bleeding or infection) are low with proper care and higher when care is unsafe or delayed.

### Post-abortion care

Includes follow-up, treating complications, contraception counselling and emotional support. People’s emotional experiences vary widely.

### Not personal advice

See a registered doctor for your situation. Source: [WHO Abortion fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/abortion).

## Abortion Law in India

### Up to 20 weeks

Termination is allowed on the opinion of one registered medical practitioner, on grounds specified in the MTP Act (e.g. risk to health, contraceptive failure, fetal abnormality).

### 20–24 weeks

For specified categories (e.g. survivors of sexual assault, minors, change in marital status, disability, certain vulnerable women), on the opinion of two practitioners.

### Beyond 24 weeks

Only for substantial fetal abnormalities, decided by a State-level Medical Board. Emergencies endangering life are treated separately in the law.

### Privacy

The woman’s name and details may be disclosed only to persons authorised by law.

Legal access is not the same as practical access: distance, cost, provider shortages, stigma and awareness gaps can limit care, especially in rural areas.

**1860** Indian Penal Code criminalises causing miscarriage, with limited exceptions.

**1971** MTP Act passed, legalising termination under specified conditions.

**2021** MTP (Amendment) Act raises upper limit to 24 weeks for specified categories; introduces Medical Boards; widens the provision to “woman” rather than only married women.

**2022** Supreme Court, in X v. Principal Secretary, Health Dept (Delhi), held unmarried women also have access in the same 20–24 week category.

Eligibility depends on facts and statutory requirements. This is not legal advice. Read the Act on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) and see [MoHFW](https://mohfw.gov.in/).

Section 7

## Social & Economic Impact

Experiences differ greatly, so generalisations mislead. Common themes include financial and educational circumstances, stigma and discrimination, rural–urban healthcare gaps, gender inequality, and mental health. WHO notes that stigma leads to under-reporting of abortion-related harm. Non-judgmental healthcare and social support help people make informed decisions, whatever they choose.
